Finding (source-reported): Mimecast reported that the actor behind the SolarWinds supply-chain compromise accessed part of its production environment, including certificates, customer server-connection information, contact details, and encrypted or hashed credentials. The actor downloaded a limited number of source-code repositories. Mimecast recorded $0.8 million in pretax incident expenses, net of anticipated insurance recoveries, by 31 March 2021. A 2024 SEC order found that the actor used an exfiltrated Mimecast certificate to compromise five customers’ cloud platforms and accessed encrypted credentials for about 31,000 Mimecast customers and server or configuration data for about 17,000. The SEC imposed a separate $990,000 civil penalty over Mimecast’s disclosures.

References:
- [Mimecast, FY2021 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1644675/000156459021030433/mime-10k_20210331.htm), filed 27 May 2021, Item 7, “Security Incident,” pp. 43–44.
- [SEC, “In the Matter of Mimecast Limited,” Release 33-11322](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2024/33-11322.pdf), 22 October 2024, § III, ¶¶ 8 and 14–16, pp. 2–4; § IV.B, p. 6.

Finding (source-reported): The U.S. Department of Justice reported compromised Microsoft 365 email accounts in 27 U.S. Attorneys’ offices; at least 80 percent of employees in four New York districts had accounts accessed. DOJ said the actor could access all email and attachments in compromised accounts from approximately 7 May to 27 December 2020, and it notified affected account holders. DOJ’s later review traced the breach to the Orion compromise and reported that failures in email security safeguards expanded access and worsened data loss.

References:
- [U.S. Department of Justice, Office of Privacy and Civil Liberties, “Department of Justice Statement on the intrusion into the Department’s Microsoft O365 email environment”](https://www.justice.gov/opcl/department-justice-statement-intrusion-department-s-microsoft-o365-email-environment), updated 12 October 2022, introductory paragraphs, district list, and final two paragraphs.

Finding (source-reported): An SEC order reported that Check Point found infected Orion installations on two servers and SolarWinds-related activity between July and October 2020, including command-and-control communications, unauthorized software, two compromised corporate accounts, and attempted lateral movement. Check Point said its logs did not cover all activity before September, leaving the full scope undetermined; its investigation found no evidence that customer data, code, or other sensitive information had been accessed. The SEC imposed a $995,000 civil penalty after finding that Check Point’s investor risk disclosures did not reflect the specific compromise.

References:
- [SEC, “In the Matter of Check Point Software Technologies Ltd.,” Release 33-11321](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2024/33-11321.pdf), 22 October 2024, § III, ¶¶ 1 and 9–16, pp. 1–4; § IV.B, p. 6.
