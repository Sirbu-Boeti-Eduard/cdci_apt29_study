\section{Victims and business impact}
\label{sec:victims}

Three SolarWinds customers illustrate the range of outcomes.

Mimecast reported that the actor accessed part of its production environment, including certificates, customer connection information, and encrypted or hashed credentials, and downloaded a limited number of source-code repositories. By 31 March 2021 it had recorded USD~0.8 million in pretax incident expenses, net of anticipated insurance recoveries~\cite{mimecast-10k}. A 2024 SEC order found that the actor exfiltrated an authentication certificate and used it against five customers' cloud platforms, and that it accessed a database holding encrypted credentials for about 31,000 customers and server or configuration information for about 17,000. The order put the source-code loss at 58\% of Mimecast's exgestion code, 50\% of its Microsoft 365 authentication code, and 76\% of its interoperability code, and imposed a USD~990,000 penalty over the company's disclosures~\cite{sec-mimecast}.

The Department of Justice reported compromised Microsoft 365 accounts in 27 U.S.\ Attorneys' offices, with at least 80\% of employees affected in four New York districts. It treated all mail in those accounts as accessed, for a period from about 7 May to 27 December 2020~\cite{doj-statement}. Its later review traced the intrusion to Orion and found that Office 365 configuration choices widened the actor's access~\cite{doj-review}.

Check Point found infected Orion installations on two servers and activity between July and October 2020, including command and control, unauthorised software, two compromised corporate accounts, and attempted lateral movement. Its logs did not cover all activity before September, so the full scope remained undetermined, and its investigation found no evidence that customer data or code was accessed. The SEC imposed a USD~995,000 penalty over its risk disclosures~\cite{sec-checkpoint}.

Two kinds of cost recur. The first is incident response: forensics, credential resets, customer notification, and legal work. The second is disclosure risk. Mimecast and Check Point were penalised for how they described their incidents, not for being breached. For a managed service provider, the exposure also reaches customers whose credentials or certificates sat in the compromised environment, so the certificate mattered to more parties than the source code did.
